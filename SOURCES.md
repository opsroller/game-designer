# Sources

*Reference: the research this role is built on, for maintainers and reviewers.*

## Occupation frameworks and job market

- **O*NET OnLine, Video Game Designers (15-1255.01)** — https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1255.01 — official task list (design reviews, documentation, playtesting oversight, test specifications for QA); responsibilities and skills.
- **IGDA Curriculum Framework (2003)** — https://svn.sable.mcgill.ca/sable/courses/COMP763/oldpapers/IGDA-03-framework.pdf — core game design knowledge: play mechanics, balancing, feedback loops, spatial and task design, tuning, playtesting.
- **Ubisoft, Game Designer – Systems and Balancing (posting)** — https://montreal.ubisoft.com/en/jobs/game-designer-systems-and-balancing/ — progression curves, economy, balancing pipelines, turning playtest reports into tasks.
- **Eleventh Hour Games, Systems Game Designer (posting via Built In)** — https://builtin.com/job/systems-game-designer/7407550 — feature specs and system flow diagrams, statistical modelling, data-driven balance.
- **Bethesda, Senior Systems Designer (posting via Hitmarker)** — https://hitmarker.net/jobs/bethesda-senior-systems-designer-704096 — senior expectations: clear documentation, prototyping, mentoring, success criteria with analytics.
- **ResumeGeni, Game Designer Career Path** — https://resumegeni.com/blog/game-designer-career-path — junior to director ladder: scope and ownership at each level.

## Books, papers and talks

- **Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek, "MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research"** — https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf — mechanics/dynamics/aesthetics, eight aesthetics, feedback-loop tuning; basis of `systems-design`.
- **Jesse Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*** (summary at https://circa.cs.ualberta.ca/index.php/CIRCA:The_Art_of_Game_Design_-_Jesse_Schell) — experience first, problem-statement lens, elemental tetrad.
- **Katie Salen and Eric Zimmerman, *Rules of Play*** (MIT Press, 2003) — meaningful play: choices whose outcomes are discernible and integrated.
- **Tracy Fullerton, *Game Design Workshop*, USC syllabus** — https://web-app.usc.edu/soc/syllabus/20253/18358.pdf — playcentric process: experience goals, prototyping, playtesting, formal/dramatic/dynamic elements.
- **Ian Schreiber, Game Balance Concepts (lecture notes)** — https://eleganthack.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/GameBalance.pdf — four kinds of balance, cost curves, transitive and intransitive, golden rules, rule of two.
- **Ernest Adams and Joris Dormans, *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design*, ch. 5** — https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/imprint_downloads/peachpit/peachpit/samplechapters/0321820274/0321820274_gamemechanics_ch05section.pdf — Machinations: sources, drains, converters, traders, internal economy simulation.
- **Daniel Cook, "Loops and Arcs"** — https://lostgarden.com/2012/04/30/loops-and-arcs — loops build mastery, arcs deliver content once; analysing what repeats.
- **Steve Swink, *Game Feel*** (review: https://lizengland.com/blog/review-game-feel-by-steve-swink/) — real-time control, simulated space, polish; ~100 ms response threshold; feel as metrics.
- **Pichlmair and Johansen, "Designing Game Feel: A Survey"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09201 — physicality, amplification, support; coyote time, buffering, hit stop; moderate juice.
- **Kyle Pittman, "Math for Game Programmers: Building a Better Jump" (GDC 2016)** — https://gdcvault.com/play/1023148/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Building — specifying motion from designer intent (height, distance) rather than raw physics.
- **Stone Librande, "One-Page Designs" (GDC 2010)** — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-the-goal-of-design-is-to-efficiently-communicate-ideas- — short, visual, distilled design documents.
- **Dan Taylor, "Ten Principles for Good Level Design" (GDC 2013)** — https://andrewdowell.artstation.com/blog/RLzG0/ten-principles-for-good-level-design — mechanics drive levels, start from emotion, clear goals, keep teaching, risk and reward.
- **The Level Design Book, "Process" and "Blockout"** — https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/overview — the industry level pipeline: brief, layout, blockout, playtest, then art; blockouts are cheap to change and must be tested before decoration. Basis of `author-level` (critical path first, test before polish).
- **The Level Design Book, "Playtesting" (blockout)** — https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/playtesting — the author is the worst tester of their own level; watch fresh players. Basis of the fresh-reviewer check in `author-level`.
- **Koichi Hayashida on Mario level design (Game Developer)** — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design — introduce, develop, twist, conclude: one idea per stage, used to check a level against its brief.
- **Gillian Smith and Jim Whitehead, "Analyzing the Expressive Range of a Level Generator" (PCG Workshop 2010)** — https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/smith2010analyzing.pdf — measuring levels with computed metrics (linearity, leniency) rather than by eye; basis of validating authored data automatically and comparing it with targets.

## Playtesting practice

- **Mike Ambinder, "Valve's Approach to Playtesting" (GDC 2009)** — https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf — designs as hypotheses, observation over opinion, methods and their biases.
- **Games User Research, "Games User Research Methods"** — https://gamesuserresearch.com/games-user-research-methods/ — when to use observation, interviews, surveys, telemetry; pitfalls.
- **MeasuringU, "Ten Things to Know about the RITE Method"** — https://measuringu.com/rite-method/ — Medlock et al.'s RITE: fix between sessions, verify with change-free sessions.

- **Diátaxis** — https://diataxis.fr — the four documentation kinds and the compass, applied via the project's Diátaxis guide.
